Research note · Statistical arbitrage

No detectable edge: cointegration pairs trading on the Russell 3000, 2023–2025

The v33 design trades same-industry cointegrated pairs among the 500 most liquid point-in-time Russell 3000 members, re-formed every six months, with next-day fills and a per-stock cost model. Over 2023–2025 it shows no detectable edge, before or after costs; a second strategy family agrees; and a pre-registered test on 2026 data (sealed, not prospective), filed on 26 October 2026 and run once the same day, rejects zero for neither tested arm (Section 1).

Navnoor Bawa · v33 design, development run of 21 October 2026 · Note updated 26 October 2026 · Hypothetical backtest

Key findings

No detectable edge
  1. No detectable edge in the development period, before or after costs. From July 2023 to December 2025 the v33 design returned −0.39% net (Sharpe −0.06; 95% interval −1.29 to +1.16); it cannot be told apart from zero, from the textbook distance method or from random pairs. Evidence: Exhibit 2, 6, 7
  2. The same rule started earlier lost money. Started in February 2023, the walk-forward has a Sharpe ratio of −1.04 and a one-sided 95% upper bound of −0.15. Evidence: Exhibit 3, 6
  3. Costs exceed what the trades made, and the selected mean reversion does not persist. Before costs the trades made $1.52M against $1.91M of modelled costs; the rule earns +64.7 bp a trade on the data that selected its pairs and −12.6 bp after formation. Evidence: Exhibit 9, 12
  4. A second strategy family agrees. Avellaneda–Lee residual reversion, on the same universe rule, fills and costs, has a Sharpe ratio of −0.28 over 10,916 trades. Evidence: Exhibit 22
  5. The search finds nothing. None of the 38 variants declared before any ran beats cash, even before the search is allowed for (best t = 0.38; Hansen's SPA p = 0.964), and choosing among them would overfit (probability of backtest overfitting 79.4%). Evidence: Exhibit 8
  6. The pre-registered 2026 test rejects neither null. Sealed, not prospective (the project was developed during the test period), it was filed before any 2026 daily price history was fetched into the project and run once on 26 October 2026: Holm-adjusted p = 0.955 and 0.955. With 16% power against a Sharpe ratio of 1, stated in advance, that is not evidence that the edge is zero. Evidence: Section 1, Exhibit 1
  7. What the development sample can and cannot say. The main backtest rules out a Sharpe ratio above 0.92 (one-sided 95% bound) but cannot tell 0 from 0.5, and the published start date is a favourable path. Evidence: Exhibit 6, the start date

The bottom line, in full

every number behind the findings, in two paragraphs

No detectable edge, before or after costs. From July 2023 to December 2025 the v33 design returned −0.39% net on its capital (Sharpe −0.06; 95% interval −1.29 to +1.16). The same rule started in February 2023, the walk-forward, lost money: Sharpe −1.04 (−2.09 to +0.05), a one-sided 95% upper bound of −0.15 and an eight-factor alpha of −2.59% a year (t = −2.25), though the Sharpe ratio's two-sided bootstrap interval just includes zero. Before costs the 213 trades made $1.52M (Sharpe +0.29, Newey–West p = 0.645), 0.80 times the $1.91M of modelled costs: +13.5 bp of notional a trade weighted by size, −7.9 bp equal-weighted (95% interval −68.7 to +52.9), against 16.9 bp of cost (16.1 bp for the average trade). The gross return cannot be told apart from zero or from the cost, and $782.5k of it is market exposure.

The evidence layer is consistent with this. The same rule on the same pairs earns +64.7 bp a trade on the data that selected them (t = 8.35) and −12.6 bp after formation (t = −1.17); after formation the spreads reach their profit targets about as often as a random walk would (72 targets against 65.9, not the 121.0 the selected mean reversion implies); and on the strategy's own candidate pairs, 62 of 1,196 pairs cointegrated in one year are cointegrated the next (5.18%, against a base rate of 5.55%). A second family, Avellaneda–Lee residual reversion on the same universe rule and costs, agrees: Sharpe −0.28 (−1.47 to +0.93) over 10,916 trades. Of 38 variants of the design declared before any ran, 7 have a positive Sharpe ratio (best +0.21); none beats cash, even before the search is allowed for (best t = 0.38; Hansen's SPA p = 0.964), or the distance method on a Sharpe scale (SPA p = 0.489); and the variant best on one half of the days ranks below the median on the other half in 79.4% of splits, where a ranking without information would do so in half: choosing parameters on this family would overfit. The headline is also a favourable path: against the same rule started on each of 11 later dates a month apart, the published run has the higher Sharpe ratio on that start's own days in 10 (median gap 0.51), and the design ranks 9th of the 38 variants (median −0.40). The sample rules out a Sharpe ratio above 0.92 (one-sided 95% bound) but cannot tell 0 from 0.5. The pre-registered falsification test on 2026 data, filed before any 2026 daily price history was fetched into the project and run once from the registered commit 7c7d3f9, rejects zero for neither tested arm: over 200 days the v33 pairs design returned +0.08% net (Sharpe +0.05) and the second family −1.59% (Sharpe −0.27), with Holm-adjusted p-values of 0.955 and 0.955. The test had 16% power against a true Sharpe ratio of 1, stated in advance, so not rejecting the nulls is not evidence that the edge is zero: the test rules out only Sharpe ratios above 1.64 and 1.38 (one-sided 95% bounds; Section 1).

1The pre-registered 2026 test Complete

In brief Neither null is rejected. Over 200 days S1, the v33 pairs design, returned +0.08% net (Sharpe +0.05) and S2, Avellaneda–Lee, −1.59% (Sharpe −0.27), with Holm-adjusted p-values of 0.955 and 0.955; with 16% power against a Sharpe ratio of 1, that is not evidence that the edge is zero.

Every design choice in this study was made on 2020–2025 data, over 33 versions, so the development period cannot test those choices. The pre-registered confirmatory test ran on daily prices that no part of the code or its caches had held before it: those of the 200 trading days from 2 January to 19 October 2026. That period had ended before the filing, and the project was developed during it, from version 8 to version 33: the test is sealed, not prospective, and the designers' general knowledge of 2026 markets cannot be sealed. No daily price history of any security for dates after 31 December 2025 was fetched into the project's code, caches or files before the filing (network checks that asked for one stock's five-day chart discarded the answer unread), and from commit a1c76db of 20 October 2026, five hours after the 2026 calendar was added to the code, the fetch code refused such a request until the registered commit unsealed it; the other 2026 information the project received (each security's latest price and 52-week high and low in the vendor's responses, the market values and last sale prices of the stock screener behind the v32 universe file, identity data, factor files that include the short-term reversal factor, and the rate, VIX and industry series, ETF pages, index changes, short-interest and ticker lists that surveys of free data sources downloaded) is listed in Section 11 and in the pre-registration. The record that no such price history was fetched begins with the repository's first commit (12 June 2026, version 25): every committed version of the fetch code ended its requests at 31 December 2025 until the 2026 calendar was added. Versions 8 to 24, developed from 20 February 2026, left no code or session logs; only the engineering log covers them, and it records their price data ending on 30 December 2025. The protocol was filed first, on 26 October 2026; the runbook then ran the three arms and the evaluation, every attempt is on record (run record, below), and the result is published in full, as the protocol requires.

S3, and the data step's top-up of its symbols, therefore read a universe file rebuilt on 26 October 2026, before the filing, from results/v32_dev/price_fetch_manifest.json: the same 2,999 symbols in one column, without market values (sha256 f1acb6a5…, pinned in reference/holdout_inputs.sha256), where both rehearsals read the original (34661d82…).

The power was stated in advance, from no return dated after 31 December 2025. With returns shaped like S1's development returns, a test at Holm's first step (one-sided 2.5%) over 200 days detects with 80% probability only an annualised Sharpe ratio of about 2.96 (3.15 for normal returns), and its power against a true Sharpe ratio of 1, 2 and 3 is 16%, 48% and 81%. The holdout can falsify a large edge; it cannot confirm a small one, and not rejecting the null does not show that the edge is zero, which is why each arm's one-sided upper bound is reported. The pre-registration also discloses the test's simulated size at this length, which departs slightly from the nominal level. Running v32 alone first would spend the holdout, so the three arms were frozen together and ran under one runbook (run record, above).

The start date #

Because positions are sized on current equity and the 30% cap binds, which signals the book can take depends on what it already holds, so the start date is a free parameter of the result. It was measured directly: the main backtest re-run from 12 start dates 21 trading days apart, with the same pairs, re-formations, rule and costs (the published start reproduces the published run to the cent). Every later start covers a shorter window, from 607 days down to 397, and misses the first months of the published one, so each is compared with the published path on its own days. On those days the published run has the higher Sharpe ratio in 10 of the 11 cases (median gap 0.51; its own Sharpe ratio on those windows runs from −0.34 to −0.01): the published path is a favourable one. On the 397 days all twelve share, from 3 June 2024, it ranks 3rd of the twelve (−0.08, against a median of −0.47), and the same pairs on the same days give Sharpe ratios from −0.74 to +0.48, depending only on each run's own history: the positions it held, and the trade record and volatility that set its sizes and pair cool-offs. Unmatched, over windows of different length, the published start's Sharpe ratio of −0.06 is the highest of the twelve, which run down to −1.16 with a median of −0.65 (returns from −6.27% to −0.39%). Run as separate backtests, the first half of the entry period (to 30 September 2024) has a Sharpe ratio of +0.11 and the second half −0.85; inside the continuous run the same days give +0.22 and −0.31 (the first half's separate run includes 23 days in which its open positions run off). None of these runs is a new design, and none is a trial in the registry (Section 7).

3Is it significant?

Not in the strategy's favour: every bootstrap interval below contains zero, gross or net, in both strategy families, and the tests that reject, the walk-forward's one-sided Sharpe test and its eight-factor alpha (t = −2.25, Section 6), reject in the direction of a loss. The more useful question is what the data can rule out. With 628 daily observations the standard error of the annualised Sharpe ratio is 0.59, allowing for the returns' fat tails and autocorrelation, so the one-sided 95% upper bound is 0.92, and a true Sharpe ratio would need to be about 1.47 to be detected with 80% probability. A Sharpe ratio of 1.0 is rejected (p = 0.037); 0.5 is not (p = 0.172). An observed Sharpe ratio of 0.5 becomes significant only after about 9.5 years of daily data, and a strategy whose true Sharpe ratio is 0.5 reaches that bar only about half the time; at 1.0 the length is 2.4 years. Multiple-testing hurdles sit higher still: a Sharpe ratio of 1.75 for a one-sided Bonferroni correction over the 32 recorded versions, and the field-wide hurdles of Harvey, Liu and Zhu (t = 3.00, a Sharpe ratio of 1.78) and of Chordia, Goyal and Saretto (t = 3.80, 2.26).

The search, tested #

The entry, exit and stop thresholds of this design were chosen across versions on this same data, so the question is not whether one variant looks good but whether the best of those tried beats a benchmark once the search is allowed for. On 20 October 2026, before any variant ran, the study declared a family of 38 variants around the v33 design: a 27-point grid of the entry, exit and stop thresholds and 11 one-at-a-time toggles of design switches, most of them tried in earlier versions (the regime gate, entry gates, the correlation floor, the z-score window, the learned ranker, open fills, pairs across industries, v32's universe size, quarterly re-formation). Each ran once on the development days; the design is not re-chosen from the results. 7 of the 38 have a positive Sharpe ratio, the best +0.21 (the 300-name universe) and the median −0.40; the v33 design ranks 9th. No variant beats cash, even before the search is allowed for: the best variant's studentized mean is t = 0.38, Hansen's SPA test gives p = 0.964 and White's Reality Check p = 0.977, and the Romano–Wolf stepdown rejects 0 variants. No variant is shown to beat the Gatev distance method either, itself at a Sharpe ratio of −0.80 on these days: with every series scaled to unit volatility, a Sharpe scale, SPA p = 0.489 (the best variant's t = 1.35 before allowing for the search); on daily returns, whose capital bases differ, p = 0.219 (t = 1.54). The model confidence set keeps all 38: the data cannot single out a best variant. And the ranking does not persist. Cut the 628 days into 10 consecutive blocks and take each of the 252 ways of choosing half of them as the selection half: the variant best on the selection half ranks below the median on the other half in 79.4% of splits, where a ranking without information would do so in half of them (the probability of backtest overfitting), and its Sharpe ratio there averages −0.87, against −0.53 for the median variant. It loses money there in 99.2% of splits; a variant picked without looking loses in 77%, so most of that loss rate is the family's own, and selection adds to it. Choosing parameters on this family would overfit: the differences among the variants cannot be told apart from noise.

4Why no edge is detected

No gross edge is detected. Before costs the 213 trades made $1,520.1k; modelled costs were $1,907.4k, 67.4% of it market impact, so costs would have to fall to 0.80 times their modelled level for net P&L to break even. Weighted by size, the trades earned +13.5 bp of notional before costs against 16.9 bp of cost; equal-weighted, the average trade earned −7.9 bp (t = −0.25) against 16.1 bp. On either basis the gross return is indistinguishable from zero and from the cost. Four measurements of the evidence layer are consistent with one mechanism: the rule's edge on the data that selected a pair disappears afterwards (Exhibit 12); after formation the spreads move like random walks between the target and the stop (Exhibit 11), with half-lives longer than the rule allows (Exhibit 14); and the traded pairs, like the strategy's own candidate pairs and every pair of the 300 most liquid names, stay cointegrated only at about the base rate (Exhibit 13 and the table below it).

5Execution and costs

In brief Under every declared cost and capacity scenario the full re-run loses money; with the trades held fixed, the Sharpe ratio crosses zero only if costs fall to 0.83× their modelled level.

Every fill pays its own costs, from the stock's own liquidity on that day, and fills at the next day's close: a signal seen at the close of day t trades in the closing auction of day t+1. The average trade held 15.1 trading days, and its larger leg was 1.61% of that stock's full-day dollar ADV; the closing auction trades only a fraction of a day's volume, so participation in the auction itself is higher. Annual turnover was 9.0× capital. The cost model was fixed before the run, and its uncertain parts are stressed in scenarios declared in advance.

6Risk and attribution

In brief Returns load on short-term reversal (t = 4.01) and the market (t = 2.37), as a convergence strategy's should, and leave no positive alpha: −0.51% a year in the main backtest (t = −0.38).

The book's factor exposures are the ones a convergence strategy built from hedge ratios below one should have; Chen, Chen, Chen and Li (2019) find the profits of a correlation-based pairs strategy largely explained by short-term reversal and one-month industry momentum. Against the Fama–French five factors, momentum and short- and long-term reversal, with Newey–West errors, the main backtest loads on short-term reversal (t = 4.01), on the market (t = 2.37) and against momentum (t = −3.96). The eight factors explain R² = 0.158 of its daily variance and leave an alpha of −0.51% a year (t = −0.38). The walk-forward loads the same way (short-term reversal t = 3.80) and leaves an alpha of −2.59% a year (t = −2.25). Against an equal-weight sector-ETF proxy the main backtest's beta is 0.037 (t = 2.40).

8Second strategy family: residual reversion

In brief The second family agrees: Avellaneda–Lee residual reversion, on the same universe rule, fills and costs, returned −3.37% (Sharpe −0.28) over 10,916 trades, earning +5.35 bp a trade before costs against 11.30 bp of cost.

A null on one rule says little about the idea. Avellaneda and Lee (2010) trade each stock's residual against its sector ETF when that residual, modelled as a mean-reverting process, is stretched; many small concurrent bets give it far more breadth than 213 pair trades, though over the same 628 days its Sharpe ratio is measured no more precisely (95% interval −1.47 to +0.93, against −1.29 to +1.16 for the pairs strategy). The parameters are the paper's: a 60-day regression on the sector ETF, an AR(1) fit of the cumulative residual, trades only when mean reversion is faster than 30 days, entry at |s| > 1.25, exits at −0.50 (long) and 0.75 (short). Fills, costs and the universe rule are the pairs strategy's: next-day closes, the same cost model, and the 500 most liquid point-in-time members, re-ranked every 21 trading days. Over the same period it made 10,916 trades, held 181 positions on average and returned −3.37% (Sharpe −0.28; Newey–West p = 0.598). Before costs it earned +5.35 bp a trade against 11.30 bp of cost: 9.89 bp on the stock legs and 1.41 bp on the ETF hedge. Treating the trades as independent gives t = 0.87, but trades open at the same time share their market moves: with standard errors clustered by entry month (30 clusters) t is 0.63 (p = 0.53), the variance of the mean is 1.9 times the independent one, and clustering by entry date or by stock gives 0.81 and 0.86. Net of its own stock-leg costs the mean trade returned −4.54 bp (t = −0.53 clustered by month); the ETF hedge's further 1.41 bp a trade is booked on the netted ETF book. This universe rule is the arm's third: the pairs strategy's screens replaced the previous rule after a review measured the P&L of the names they remove (Section 7). Those names had lost money before costs over the development period, so the change may flatter this arm's development result.

9Design and method

Selection sees formation data only; every trading decision on day t uses data up to day t and fills on day t+1; the evidence is computed after the run. Tests change future prices and check that no past decision moves. The v33 design differs from the published v32 design in seven fields. Each change addresses a defect found in v32's design or in its development run on this same period, so the changes were made after v32's development results were seen; none re-tunes a trading threshold, and all were fixed before any v33 result was seen (the exploratory and the published v33 runs share one design hash, Section 7).

12Literature

Published results for the same strategy families, as reported by their authors. Most point the same way: the classic pairs edge was large before the 2000s, has shrunk since, and is small after realistic costs; where it survives, it depends on why the two prices diverged, and much of it is short-term reversal and industry momentum.

13Reproduce

Every number on this page is either a marked value checked against the run's headline JSON or computed from the committed results by one script, scripts/build_site_data.py. The runbooks clear every PAIRS_* switch, fix the hash seed, refuse an environment that differs from requirements.txt and record the commit in every export. A later price fetch will not match the cached data bit for bit, because vendors revise history; the run manifest pins what was used.

# the environment pinned in requirements.txt (Python 3.12)
python3.12 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt

# the development-period studies behind this note, in order:
