Research note · Statistical arbitrage

No detectable edge: cointegration pairs trading on the Russell 3000, 2023–2025

The v33 design trades same-industry cointegrated pairs among the 500 most liquid point-in-time Russell 3000 members, re-formed every six months, with next-day fills and a per-stock cost model. Over 2023–2025 it shows no positive edge: the main backtest cannot be told apart from zero, before or after costs, from the textbook distance method or from random pairs; the same rule started earlier lost money; none of 38 variants declared in advance beats cash or the distance method once the search is allowed for; and a second strategy family agrees. A pre-registered test on 2026 data is sealed until its protocol is filed.

Navnoor Bawa · v33 design, development run of 21 October 2026 · Note updated 21 October 2026 · Hypothetical backtest

Bottom line

No detectable edge

No detectable edge, before or after costs. From July 2023 to December 2025 the v33 design returned −0.39% net on its capital (Sharpe −0.06; 95% interval −1.29 to +1.16). The same rule started in February 2023, the walk-forward, lost money: Sharpe −1.04 (−2.09 to +0.05), a one-sided 95% upper bound of −0.15 and an eight-factor alpha with t = −2.25, though its two-sided interval just includes zero. Before costs the 213 trades made $1.52M (Sharpe +0.29, Newey–West p = 0.645), 0.80 times the $1.91M of modelled costs: +13.5 bp of notional a trade weighted by size, −7.9 bp equal-weighted (95% interval −68.7 to +52.9), against 16.9 bp of cost (16.1 bp for the average trade). The gross return cannot be told apart from zero or from the cost, and $782.5k of it is market exposure.

The evidence layer is consistent with this. The same rule on the same pairs earns +64.7 bp a trade on the data that selected them (t = 8.35) and −12.6 bp after formation (t = −1.17). After formation the spreads reach their profit targets about as often as a random walk would (72 targets against 65.9), not as often as the selected mean reversion implies (121.0), and 6 of the 115 traded pairs testable after formation are still cointegrated, about the rate of any correlated pair: on the 300 most liquid names of 2022, pairs cointegrated in one year are cointegrated the next at 5.04% against a base rate of 5.50%, itself close to the test's 5% level. A second family, Avellaneda–Lee residual reversion on the same universe rule and costs, agrees: Sharpe −0.28 (−1.47 to +0.93) over 10,916 trades. Of 38 variants of the design declared before any ran, 7 have a positive Sharpe ratio (best +0.21), none beats cash once the search is allowed for (Hansen's SPA p = 0.964) or the distance method (p = 0.219), and the variant that is best on one half of the days loses money on the other half in 99.2% of splits: choosing parameters on this family would overfit. The headline Sharpe ratio is also the most favourable of 12 start dates a month apart (median −0.65). The sample rules out a Sharpe ratio above 0.92 (one-sided 95% bound) but cannot tell 0 from 0.5. The pre-registered falsification test on 2026 data, which has 16% power against a true Sharpe ratio of 1, stays sealed until its pre-registration is filed.

1The pre-registered 2026 test Sealed

Every design choice in this study was made on 2020–2025 data, over 33 versions, so the development period cannot test those choices. The pre-registered confirmatory test runs on data that no part of the code or its caches has held: the 200 trading days from 2 January to 19 October 2026. That period has already ended, and the project was developed during it, from version 8 to version 33: the test is sealed, not prospective, and the designers' general knowledge of 2026 markets cannot be sealed. No daily price history of any security for dates after 31 December 2025 has been requested, and the fetch code refuses such a request until the registered commit unseals it; the other 2026 information the project received (the latest quote in the vendor's responses, the market values behind the v32 universe file, identity data, factor files) is listed in Section 11 and will be listed in the pre-registration. The protocol is filed first; each arm then runs once, and its result is published whatever it is.

The power is stated before any 2026 daily price is seen. With returns shaped like the development period's, a test at Holm's first step (one-sided 2.5%) over 200 days detects with 80% probability only an annualised Sharpe ratio of about 2.96 (3.15 for normal returns), and its power against a true Sharpe ratio of 1, 2 and 3 is 16%, 48% and 81%. The holdout can falsify a large edge; it cannot confirm a small one, and not rejecting the null will not show that the edge is zero, which is why each arm's one-sided upper bound is reported. The pre-registration also discloses the test's simulated size at this length, which departs slightly from the nominal level. Running v32 alone first would spend the holdout, so the three arms are frozen together and each runs once.

The start is the development calendar's, which v32 set after the label window of its ranking model (to 30 June 2023); v33 has no ranker but keeps the calendar, so that the two designs are compared on the same dates. The start date

Because positions are sized on current equity and the 30% cap binds, which signals the book can take depends on what it already holds, so the start date is a free parameter of the result. It was measured directly: the main backtest re-run from 12 start dates 21 trading days apart, with the same pairs, re-formations, rule and costs (the published start reproduces the published run to the cent). The published start, 3 July 2023, is the most favourable of the twelve: its Sharpe ratio of −0.06 is the highest, the others run down to −1.16, and the median is −0.65 (returns from −6.27% to −0.39%). On the 397 days all twelve share, from 3 June 2024, the same pairs on the same days give Sharpe ratios from −0.74 to +0.48, depending only on the book each run already held. Run as separate backtests, the first half of the entry period (to 30 September 2024) has a Sharpe ratio of +0.11 and the second half −0.85; inside the continuous run the same days give +0.22 and −0.31 (the first half's separate run includes 23 days in which its open positions run off). None of these runs is a new design, and none is a trial in the registry (Section 7).

3Is it significant?

Not in the strategy's favour: every bootstrap interval below contains zero, gross or net, in both strategy families, and the one test that rejects, the walk-forward's one-sided test, rejects in the direction of a loss. The more useful question is what the data can rule out. With 628 daily observations the standard error of the annualised Sharpe ratio is 0.59, allowing for the returns' fat tails and autocorrelation, so the one-sided 95% upper bound is 0.92, and a true Sharpe ratio would need to be about 1.47 to be detected with 80% probability. A Sharpe ratio of 1.0 is rejected (p = 0.037); 0.5 is not (p = 0.172). An observed Sharpe ratio of 0.5 becomes significant only after about 9.5 years of daily data, and a strategy whose true Sharpe ratio is 0.5 reaches that bar only about half the time; at 1.0 the length is 2.4 years. Multiple-testing hurdles sit higher still: a Sharpe ratio of 1.75 for a one-sided Bonferroni correction over the 32 recorded versions, and the field-wide hurdles of Harvey, Liu and Zhu (t = 3.00, a Sharpe ratio of 1.78) and of Chordia, Goyal and Saretto (t = 3.80, 2.26).

The search, tested

The entry, exit and stop thresholds of this design were chosen across versions on this same data, so the question is not whether one variant looks good but whether the best of those tried beats a benchmark once the search is allowed for. On 20 October 2026, before any variant ran, the study declared a family of 38 variants around the v33 design: a 27-point grid of the entry, exit and stop thresholds and 11 one-at-a-time toggles of design switches, most of them tried in earlier versions (the regime gate, entry gates, the correlation floor, the z-score window, the learned ranker, open fills, pairs across industries, v32's universe size, quarterly re-formation). Each ran once on the development days; the design is not re-chosen from the results. 7 of the 38 have a positive Sharpe ratio, the best +0.21 (the 300-name universe) and the median −0.40; the v33 design ranks 9th. No variant beats cash once the search is allowed for: Hansen's SPA test gives p = 0.964 and White's Reality Check p = 0.977, the best variant's studentized mean is t = 0.38, and the Romano–Wolf stepdown rejects 0 variants. None beats the Gatev distance method either (SPA p = 0.219; 0.489 on a Sharpe scale). The model confidence set keeps all 38: the data cannot single out a best variant. And the ranking does not persist: split the days into halves in each of the 252 balanced ways, and the variant best on one half ranks below the median on the other in 79.4% of splits (the probability of backtest overfitting) and loses money there in 99.2%. Choosing parameters on this family would overfit: the differences among the variants cannot be told apart from noise.

4Why no edge is detected

No gross edge is detected. Before costs the 213 trades made $1,520.1k; modelled costs were $1,907.4k, 67.4% of it market impact, so costs would have to fall to 0.80 times their modelled level for net P&L to break even. Weighted by size, the trades earned +13.5 bp of notional before costs against 16.9 bp of cost; equal-weighted, the average trade earned −7.9 bp (t = −0.25) against 16.1 bp. On either basis the gross return is indistinguishable from zero and from the cost. Four measurements of the evidence layer are consistent with one mechanism: the rule's edge on the data that selected a pair disappears afterwards (Exhibit 12); after formation the spreads move like random walks between the target and the stop (Exhibit 11), with half-lives longer than the rule allows (Exhibit 14); and the traded pairs, like the strategy's own candidate pairs and pairs on a broader universe, stay cointegrated only at about the base rate (Exhibit 13 and the table below it).

5Execution and costs

Every fill pays its own costs, from the stock's own liquidity on that day, and fills at the next day's close: a signal seen at the close of day t trades in the closing auction of day t+1. The average trade held 15.1 trading days, and its larger leg was 1.61% of that stock's full-day dollar ADV; the closing auction trades only a fraction of a day's volume, so participation in the auction itself is higher. Annual turnover was 9.0× capital. The cost model was fixed before the run, and its uncertain parts are stressed in scenarios declared in advance.

6Risk and attribution

The book's factor exposures are the ones a convergence strategy built from hedge ratios below one should have; Chen, Chen, Chen and Li (2019) find the profits of a correlation-based pairs strategy largely explained by short-term reversal and one-month industry momentum. Against the Fama–French five factors, momentum and short- and long-term reversal, with Newey–West errors, the main backtest loads on short-term reversal (t = 4.01), on the market (t = 2.37) and against momentum (t = −3.96). The eight factors explain R² = 0.158 of its daily variance and leave an alpha of −0.51% a year (t = −0.38). The walk-forward loads the same way (short-term reversal t = 3.80) and leaves an alpha of −2.59% a year (t = −2.25). Against an equal-weight sector-ETF proxy the main backtest's beta is 0.037 (t = 2.40).

Regimes

Do and Faff (2010) find that pairs trading does best in prolonged turbulence, and the study's 30 months are a single period, so the net returns were split by the previous day's VIX close and by the previous day's cross-sectional dispersion of the 11 sector-ETF returns, each into terciles of the backtest's own days. No split separates the returns: the mean daily return is −0.56 bp in the lowest VIX tercile (VIX below 14.72) and +0.36 bp in the highest (above 17.21), a difference of +0.92 bp a day with a Newey–West t of 0.74 (p = 0.46); by dispersion the difference is −0.35 bp (t = −0.31). The terciles are cut on the backtest's own days, so the split describes the period and is not a rule that could have been traded; the gross returns split the same way.

7Multiple testing

At the first formation, 5,082 same-industry pairs were tested and 489 were “cointegrated” at p < 0.05, against 254.1 expected by chance alone. None survives a Benjamini–Hochberg correction at q = 0.05 (1 at q = 0.10): the excess is spread thinly over many weak relationships. Selection kept 309 pairs at that formation, which governed no trade: it was replaced at the first session after the cutoff, before trading began. Across the 6 formations that governed trading, 130 distinct pairs were traded. Beyond the pairs, every trial whose result has been seen is on record.

The start-date and half-period runs of the robustness checks (Section 2) re-run the published configuration from other start dates; they are not new designs and are not registered as trials.

8Second strategy family: residual reversion

A null on one rule says little about the idea. Avellaneda and Lee (2010) trade each stock's residual against its sector ETF when that residual, modelled as a mean-reverting process, is stretched; many small concurrent bets give it far more breadth than 213 pair trades, though over the same 628 days its Sharpe ratio is measured no more precisely (95% interval −1.47 to +0.93, against −1.29 to +1.16 for the pairs strategy). The parameters are the paper's: a 60-day regression on the sector ETF, an AR(1) fit of the cumulative residual, trades only when mean reversion is faster than 30 days, entry at |s| > 1.25, exits at −0.50 (long) and 0.75 (short). Fills, costs and the universe rule are the pairs strategy's: next-day closes, the same cost model, and the 500 most liquid point-in-time members, re-ranked every 21 trading days. Over the same period it made 10,916 trades, held 181 positions on average and returned −3.37% (Sharpe −0.28; Newey–West p = 0.598). Before costs it earned +5.35 bp a trade against 11.30 bp of cost. Treating the trades as independent gives t = 0.87, but trades open at the same time share their market moves: with standard errors clustered by entry month (30 clusters) t is 0.63 (p = 0.53), the variance of the mean is 1.9 times the independent one, and clustering by entry date or by stock gives 0.81 and 0.86. Net of costs the mean trade lost −4.54 bp (t = −0.53 clustered by month).

9Design and method

Selection sees formation data only; every trading decision on day t uses data up to day t and fills on day t+1; the evidence is computed after the run. Tests change future prices and check that no past decision moves. The v33 design differs from the published v32 design in seven fields. Each change addresses a defect found in v32's design or in its development run on this same period, so the changes were made after v32's development results were seen; none re-tunes a trading threshold, and all were fixed before any v33 result was seen (the exploratory and the published v33 runs share one design hash, Section 7).

A backtest on real data says what a strategy did, not whether the code measures what it claims. scripts/synthetic_validation.py runs the real engine, with its full cost model, on synthetic markets whose answer is known, 10 markets per scenario; CI runs a smaller version on every push to main and on pull requests.

12Literature

Published results for the same strategy families, as reported by their authors. They point the same way: the classic pairs edge was large before the 2000s, has shrunk since, and is small after realistic costs; where it survives, it depends on why the two prices diverged, and much of it is short-term reversal.

13Reproduce

Every number on this page is either a marked value checked against the run's headline JSON or computed from the committed results by one script, scripts/build_site_data.py. The runbooks clear every PAIRS_* switch, fix the hash seed, refuse an environment that differs from requirements.txt and record the commit in every export. A later price fetch will not match the cached data bit for bit, because vendors revise history; the run manifest pins what was used.
