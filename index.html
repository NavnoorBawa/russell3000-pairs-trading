Pairs trading research, measured honestly.

Status · v32 · October 2026

v32 rebuilt the engine. The published v31.1 figures are withdrawn until the study is re-run.

A complete audit of v31.1 (about 270 findings across seven review areas, nearly all confirmed by an independent adversarial re-check) found defects in the trading engine itself: entry and exit used different z-scores; the Kalman “hedge ratio” was a price-level ratio that changed with the data vendor’s back-adjustment; each trade’s final P&L reached the position sizer and the loss cool-off on the day it was opened; P&L was booked on exit days only; and the walk-forward silently discarded trades still open at a window’s end. Some of these defects flattered the old result and others changed the strategy itself, so the net direction cannot be predicted. The v31.1 numbers are therefore withdrawn, not adjusted.

v32 has not yet been re-run on the study data (the universe file is not redistributable and the price cache is local to the author). It is validated instead on synthetic markets with known answers, by 300+ hermetic tests (89% line coverage) and by an end-to-end run of the full pipeline in CI. Every v32 result on this page shows “—” until the next study run fills it.

A research system built to answer one question honestly: does a cointegration pairs-trading strategy on the Russell 3000 have an edge after realistic execution and costs, out of sample?

It selects pairs on formation data only, trades a Kalman-filtered spread with next-day fills and a per-stock cost model, validates with a continuous walk-forward simulation, and then tests whether the result is distinguishable from zero (Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe, Newey-West, block bootstrap) and from two baselines (Gatev et al. 2006 distance method, random pairs).

02 · Validation

Validation on markets with known answers

A backtest on real data can only say what a strategy did, not whether the code measures what it claims. scripts/synthetic_validation.py runs the real engine, with its full cost model, on synthetic markets whose answer is known (pairs_trading/synthetic.py); CI runs it on every push. Five independent markets per scenario. These are checks of the code on synthetic data, not results on the study data.

The test suite adds causality checks with teeth: every result dated before T is unchanged when all prices after T are replaced (the check catches a deliberately re-introduced sizer look-ahead at every cut-off tried), the same trades are taken at the same z-scores when the vendor back-adjusts history for a split after the sample, and the daily P&L series reconciles exactly with the per-trade P&L.

03 · Methodology

Architecture (v32)

One pipeline from universe to evidence. 18 modules in pairs_trading/; entry point python -m pairs_trading.main. Thresholds below are the shipped defaults.

universe and data

Russell 3000 constituents from data/marketcap.csv (2,542 symbols) · daily OHLCV 2020–2025 · VIX and 11 sector ETFs. Every level screen uses point-in-time (split-unadjusted) prices and volumes. A price fetch must cover at least 90% of the universe; caches are written atomically and never silently overwritten.

pair selection

Formation data to 2022-12-31 only. Symbols are screened on point-in-time price and dollar volume, and only the 300 most liquid enter the pair search, so most of the universe file is never paired. Pair tests: positive return correlation ≥ 0.25 · two-direction Engle-Granger p < 0.05 (Bonferroni across both directions) · half-life 4–25 days on the cointegrating spread · heuristic quality score. PCA (5 factors) residual cointegration adds a quality bonus. A Benjamini-Hochberg diagnostic is reported over the pairs that pass the correlation screen and are tested.

Re-selection every 90 trading days (about four months, not a calendar quarter): a 2-year re-test plus a 126-day stability check, with pair statistics re-estimated each time. Re-selection can drop pairs but not add them unless PAIRS_RESELECT=full.

spread model

Two-state Kalman filter (slope and intercept) on log prices centred on their burn-in means, so β is a hedge ratio and every signal is invariant to a vendor back-adjustment for a later split or dividend.

trading rule

One z-score for everything: z is the β-locked spread (lp₁ − βt·lp₂) against its mean and standard deviation over the last 30–60 bars (3 × half-life), frozen at the signal, and the same z drives entry, target, stop and time stop. Entry |z| ≥ 1.8 · target |z| ≤ 0.5 · stop 1.5 further against · time stop clamp(2.5 × half-life, 10, 25) days.

Execution: signal at the day-t close, fill at the t+1 close on entry and on exit; legs $1 : $β.

Per-entry gates: 20-day return correlation ≥ 0.20 · β in [0.25, 4] (outside it the signal is skipped, not clamped) · loss cool-off · ex-ante round-trip cost ≤ 1% of equity. CUSUM and Hurst entry gates exist as flags but are off by default: at 30–90 bars they reject 5–11% of both random walks and mean-reverting spreads.

ranker

Optional (PAIRS_USE_TRANSFORMER=0 disables it). A classifier of P(trade profitable) on 30 features, trained on outcomes of the actual rule and used only to order same-day candidates; its out-of-sample AUC and Brier score are reported. The order changes which trades execute only on days when candidates exceed capacity (8 entries a day, no shared legs, 30% gross cap). It sees one 30-feature vector, so it is functionally a residual MLP.

portfolio and risk

$100M · 3–10% per pair (the z multiplier applied inside that clamp) · 30% gross cap · 8 entries a day · no shared legs. Daily mark-to-market; commissions, spreads and impact booked on fill days. A drawdown and daily-loss kill switch flattens the book.

Regime scaling (the smallest applicable scale wins): VIX > 30 → 0.5×, VIX > 40 → 0.25×; sector-dispersion z > 2 → 0.5× at any VIX; VIX < 20 with dispersion z > 0.8 → 0.4×. Dispersion is the cross-sectional spread of 63-day cumulative sector returns, z-scored against its own trailing 252 days. No new entries while more than 20% of the trailing 63 days were scaled down.

costs

Per stock, per fill: $0.002/share commission · half-spread by dollar-ADV bucket · square-root impact on the stock’s own σ and dollar ADV, on entry and exit · general-collateral borrow 25 bp/yr on the short leg · prime-broker carry on the short half.

walk-forward

One continuous out-of-sample simulation on a 252-day train / 63-day test grid. The ranker is refit for each window on that window’s train period, positions carry across window boundaries, and per-window figures are calendar slices of the one simulation.

evidence layer

significance.py: Probabilistic Sharpe, Deflated Sharpe (32 trials), Newey-West t, Politis-White block bootstrap, Lo (2002) adjusted Sharpe, Ledoit-Wolf Sharpe-difference test, probability of backtest overfitting (PBO, by CSCV).

benchmark.py: Gatev (2006) distance method with t+1 fills and the same cost model · 200-draw random-pair control. Also market-beta attribution, capital utilisation and a rejection funnel.

05 · Known limitations

Known limitations

These hold for v32 and should be read with any result it produces.

  • Survivorship. The universe is close to current Russell 3000 membership back-filled with prices: of 36 names that failed, delisted or were acquired in 2020–2025, one is present. Pairs that broke because a company failed are missing, which flatters a pairs strategy. Fixing it needs a point-in-time dataset such as CRSP.
  • Design choices were made on the same data. Thresholds, gates and the regime rule were chosen across 32 versions using 2020–2025. The walk-forward is out of sample for pair selection and the ranker, not for those choices. The Deflated Sharpe counts 32 trials (a lower bound), and scripts/parameter_sensitivity.py reports how fragile the chosen thresholds are (PBO).
  • Re-selection can drop pairs but not add them unless PAIRS_RESELECT=full (slow).
  • Borrow is general-collateral for every name (25 bp/yr); hard-to-borrow fees, recalls, short rebates and regulatory fees are not modelled.
  • Fills are close-to-close; no auction, queue or intraday modelling. Capacity is reported as average ADV participation rather than simulated.
  • Ranker features include pair statistics estimated on the formation window that also produces its earliest training labels. The ranker only orders same-day candidates, and its out-of-sample AUC is reported.
  • The “transformer” sees one 30-feature vector — a sequence of length one — so it is functionally a residual MLP. Its scores are not bit-identical across CPU thread counts or devices.
  • Sector features are coarse. The sector map covers about 315 of 2,542 symbols; the rest get neutral sector inputs. Pairs are not restricted to one industry.
  • The 126-day re-selection check uses asymptotic MacKinnon p-values at n ≈ 87, which are anti-conservative; its threshold (0.30) is deliberately loose.
  • The price cache used through v31 was built with a full-sample penny-stock screen. The screen is point-in-time in v32; a cache built before that still carries the old filtering until it is re-fetched.

Literature context: Do & Faff (2010, Financial Analysts Journal; 2012, Journal of Financial Research) find that simple distance-method pairs trading became largely unprofitable after 2002 once trading costs are counted. That is indirect support for a negative result here, not a test of this strategy.

06 · Reproduce and test

Reproducing and testing

The study needs data/marketcap.csv (the study universe; required and not redistributed — the loader stops with instructions rather than substituting another universe) and the price cache, which is local to the author; a cache fetched fresh from Yahoo Finance is not guaranteed to match it. PAIRS_SEED (default 42) seeds Python, NumPy and torch and is recorded, with the git commit, in every export’s provenance block. The ranker’s scores are not bit-identical across CPU thread counts or devices.

pip install -r requirements.txt          # Python 3.12; or: pip install -e ".[dev]"
mkdir -p logs outputs

# the study (needs data/marketcap.csv; the price cache is fetched if absent)
python -m pairs_trading.main 2>&1 | tee logs/backtest.log

# publish: fill every marked number in README.md and index.html, then verify them
python scripts/check_published_numbers.py --write
python scripts/make_og_image.py          # social card from the same JSON

# evidence beyond the headline
python scripts/synthetic_validation.py   # known-answer checks (~4 min)
python scripts/parameter_sensitivity.py  # threshold grid + PBO on the study data (slow)
PAIRS_USE_TRANSFORMER=0 python -m pairs_trading.main > logs/backtest_noml.log 2>&1   # ranker ablation
PAIRS_FILL=open python -m pairs_trading.main > logs/backtest_open.log 2>&1          # fill sensitivity

Testing

pytest --cov=pairs_trading runs 300+ hermetic tests (no data files, no network; 89% line coverage, about 3 minutes). The suite runs the whole pipeline end to end on a synthetic market written in the exact on-disk format of the study, and tests the engine for causality (future-perturbation and back-adjustment invariance), accounting (daily P&L reconciles with trades; mark-to-market telescopes), the trading rule (one z definition, timing, exposure caps, run-off), the cost model, pair selection, the ranker (calibration, out-of-sample metrics, as-of features), the statistics (against reference implementations), the benchmarks, the exports and the publication scripts. CI also runs the known-answer validation, pip-audit, ruff and CodeQL; pandas/NumPy FutureWarnings are errors.

07 · Next

What happens next

To finish v32 (from the v32 entry of the engineering log):

  1. Run python -m pairs_trading.main on the study data (data/marketcap.csv and the price cache).
  2. Run python scripts/check_published_numbers.py --write, then python scripts/make_og_image.py.
  3. Write the conclusion from the numbers — v31.1’s “no edge” is not assumed.
  4. Optional: scripts/parameter_sensitivity.py (PBO of the chosen thresholds) and the PAIRS_USE_TRANSFORMER=0 / PAIRS_FILL=open sensitivity runs.

Beyond the re-run

Each of these answers a limitation listed above; none is promised to change the answer.

  • Survivorship-free data: repeat the study on a point-in-time, delisting-inclusive universe such as CRSP.
  • Untouched data for the design choices: the thresholds, gates and regime rule were chosen on 2020–2025, so only data none of the 32 versions has seen can test them out of sample.
  • Short-side and execution realism: hard-to-borrow fees, locates and recalls, short rebates, auction and intraday fills, and capacity simulated rather than reported as ADV participation.

09 · Scope

What this project is not

  • Not a deployable strategy. It is a framework for finding out whether one exists.
  • Not reinforcement learning. There is no policy, value function or environment loop; v32 deleted a loop that was labelled as training but updated nothing.
  • Not “AI-driven”. The learned component only orders candidates that the classical rule has already accepted. In v31.1 its contribution was exactly zero; v32 measures its out-of-sample skill directly.
