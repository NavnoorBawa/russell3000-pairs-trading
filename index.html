Research note · Statistical arbitrage

No detectable edge: cointegration pairs trading on the Russell 3000, 2023–2025

A pairs strategy with formation-only selection, next-day fills and a per-stock cost model, tested in a main backtest, in a continuous walk-forward and against two baselines. Its returns cannot be told apart from zero, from the textbook distance method or from random pairs. A pre-registered test on 2026 data is pending.

Navnoor Bawa · Study run v32, 18 October 2026 · Note updated 20 October 2026 · Hypothetical backtest

Bottom line

No detectable edge

From July 2023 to December 2025 the strategy returned −0.30% on its capital (Sharpe −0.07; 95% interval −1.39 to +1.23), and its continuous walk-forward out-of-sample Sharpe is −0.33 (−1.49 to +0.86). Neither differs from zero, from the Gatev distance method (difference −0.14, p = 0.879) or from random pairs (28th percentile). Before costs the average trade earned +11.4 bp of its gross notional (t = 0.37) against 12.5 bp of modelled cost, so there is no gross edge for costs to remove. The sample rules out a Sharpe ratio above 0.97 (one-sided 95% bound) but cannot tell 0 from 0.5. The decisive test, on untouched 2026 data, stays sealed until its pre-registration is filed.

1The pre-registered 2026 test Sealed

Every design choice in this study was made on 2020–2025 data over 32 versions, so the development period cannot test those choices. The decisive test therefore runs on data none of them has seen: trading days from 2 January 2026. No 2026 stock price has been fetched. The protocol is committed first; each arm then runs once, and its result is published whatever it is.

2Performance in the development period

The main backtest enters trades from 3 July 2023 to 31 December 2025 on $100M of notional capital, with pairs selected on data to 31 December 2022. Its 161 trades returned −0.30% with a maximum drawdown of −3.00%. The book held no position on 207 of 628 days and used 15.1% of capital on average (gross), against a 30% cap.

3Is it significant?

No: every interval below contains zero. The more useful question is what the data can rule out. With 628 daily observations the standard error of the annualised Sharpe ratio is 0.63, so the one-sided 95% upper bound is 0.97, and a true Sharpe ratio would need to be about 1.58 to be detected with 80% probability. A Sharpe ratio of 1.0 is rejected (p = 0.046); 0.5 is not (p = 0.184). A strategy whose true Sharpe ratio is 0.5 needs about 10.8 years of daily data to show as significant; at 1.0, about 2.7 years.

4Where the edge goes

There is no gross edge for costs to remove. Before costs the 161 trades earned $662.6k; modelled costs were $962.7k, 57.9% of it market impact. Costs would have to fall to 0.69 times their modelled level for net P&L to break even, and the gross return per trade is itself indistinguishable from zero (t = 0.37). The trades show the mechanism: the 65 stop-loss exits lost $9.80M, more than the 61 target exits earned ($8.18M).

5Execution and costs

Every fill pays its own costs, from the stock's own liquidity on that day, and fills at the next day's close: a signal seen at the close of day t trades at the close of day t+1. The average trade held 12.6 trading days, and its larger leg was 1.01% of that stock's dollar ADV. Annual turnover was 6.0× capital.

6Risk and attribution

Market exposure is small but not zero. Against an equal-weight sector-ETF proxy the main backtest's beta is 0.010 (t = 1.81) and the walk-forward's 0.016 (t = 2.17): Kalman hedge ratios below one leave most pairs net long. Annualised alpha is −0.25% in the main backtest and −0.84% out of sample. The multi-factor attribution (Fama–French five factors, momentum, short- and long-term reversal, Newey–West errors) arrives with v33.

7Multiple testing

Of 35,491 pairs tested, 2,965 were “cointegrated” at p < 0.05, against 1,775 expected by chance alone. Only 1 survives a Benjamini–Hochberg correction at q = 0.05: the excess is spread thinly over many weak relationships. Selection kept 1,776 pairs, of which 100 were traded.

8Second strategy family: residual reversion Pending

A null on one rule says little about the idea. Avellaneda and Lee (2010) trade each stock's residual against its sector ETF when that residual, modelled as a mean-reverting process, is stretched; many small concurrent bets give the test far more power than 161 pair trades. The parameters are taken from the paper unchanged: a 60-day regression on the sector ETF, an AR(1) fit of the cumulative residual, trades only when mean reversion is faster than 30 days, entry at |s| > 1.25, exits at −0.50 (long) and 0.75 (short), next-day fills and the same cost model.

9Method

Selection sees formation data only; every trading decision on day t uses data up to day t and fills on day t+1; the evidence is computed after the run. Tests change future prices and check that no past decision moves.

Specification of the v32 study run.
UniverseThe 3,000 largest US common stocks by market cap on 18 October 2026 (the Russell 3000 rule applied to the Nasdaq screener), 97.1% fetched; daily prices 2020–2025; screens on point-in-time (split-unadjusted) price and dollar volume; the 300 most liquid enter the pair search.
SelectionPositive return correlation ≥ 0.25; two-direction Engle–Granger p < 0.05 (Bonferroni across directions); half-life 4–25 days on the cointegrating spread; quality score; re-selection every 90 trading days (re-test only; pairs can drop out, not enter).
SpreadTwo-state Kalman filter (slope and intercept) on log prices centred on their burn-in means, so β is a hedge ratio and signals are invariant to vendor back-adjustment.
RuleOne β-locked z-score against its mean and standard deviation over the last 30–60 bars, frozen at the signal. Entry |z| ≥ 1.8; target |z| ≤ 0.5; stop 1.5 further against; time stop clamp(2.5 × half-life, 10, 25) days. Gates: 20-day correlation ≥ 0.20, β in [0.25, 4], loss cool-off, VIX and sector-dispersion regime scaling.
Book$100M notional; 3–10% of equity per pair; 30% gross cap; at most 8 entries a day; no shared legs; daily mark-to-market; drawdown and daily-loss kill switch.
RankerA classifier of P(trade profitable) on 30 features, used only to order same-day candidates; out-of-sample AUC 0.514 (0.510 pooled across walk-forward windows; 0.5 is no skill).
Walk-forwardOne continuous simulation on a 252-day train / 63-day test grid; the ranker is refit per window; per-window figures are calendar slices.
EvidenceProbabilistic and deflated Sharpe, Newey–West t, Politis–White block bootstrap, Lo (2002) adjustment, Ledoit–Wolf Sharpe-difference test, PBO; Gatev distance method and random pairs under the same costs and t+1 fills.

10Validation

A backtest on real data says what a strategy did, not whether the code measures what it claims. scripts/synthetic_validation.py runs the real engine, with its full cost model, on synthetic markets whose answer is known; CI runs it on every push to main and on pull requests.

The test suite adds causality checks, each confirmed by re-introducing the bug it guards against: every decision dated on or before T is unchanged when all prices, volumes and macro data after T are replaced (with classical ranking and with a trained ranker); the regime gate's inputs are prefix-invariant; the same trades occur at the same z-scores when the vendor back-adjusts history for a later split. The statistics are tested against reference implementations (statsmodels for Newey–West, arch for the Politis–White block length, an independent CSCV for PBO, the authors' code for Ledoit–Wolf).

12Literature

Published results for the same strategy families, as reported by their authors. They point the same way: the classic pairs edge was large before the 2000s, has shrunk since, and is small after realistic costs.

13Reproduce

Every number on this page is either a marked value checked against the run's headline JSON or computed from the committed results by one script. A later price fetch will not match the cached data bit for bit, because vendors revise history; the run manifest pins what was used.

# the study (prices are fetched from Yahoo's chart API and cached)
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/build_universe.py
python -m pairs_trading.main            # writes outputs/

# publish a run: copy outputs/ to results/<run>/, then
python scripts/build_site_data.py --dev results/v32_dev
python scripts/check_published_numbers.py \
    --json results/v32_dev/headline_metrics.json

# view the page locally
python -m http.server 8000
