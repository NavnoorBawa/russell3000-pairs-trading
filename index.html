Research note · Statistical arbitrage

No detectable edge: cointegration pairs trading on the Russell 3000, 2023–2025

The v33 design trades same-industry cointegrated pairs from the point-in-time Russell 3000, re-formed every six months, with next-day fills and a per-stock cost model. Over 2023–2025 its returns cannot be told apart from zero, before or after costs, from the textbook distance method or from random pairs, and a second strategy family agrees. A pre-registered test on 2026 data is sealed until its protocol is filed.

Navnoor Bawa · v33 design, development run of 21 October 2026 · Note updated 21 October 2026 · Hypothetical backtest

Bottom line

No detectable edge

No detectable edge, before or after costs. From July 2023 to December 2025 the v33 design returned −0.39% net on its capital (Sharpe −0.06; 95% interval −1.29 to +1.16), and the same rule started in February 2023, the walk-forward, has a Sharpe ratio of −1.04 (−2.09 to +0.05). Before costs the 213 trades made $1.52M against $1.91M of modelled costs; the average trade earned −7.9 bp of its notional gross (t = −0.25) against 16.1 bp of cost, so there is no gross edge for costs to remove.

The evidence layer shows why: pairs cointegrated in one year are cointegrated the next at the chance base rate (5.04% against 5.50% expected); the same rule on the same pairs earns +64.7 bp a trade on the data that selected them (t = 8.35) and −12.6 bp afterwards (t = −1.17); and out of sample the spreads reach their profit targets about as often as a random walk would (72 targets against 65.9), not as often as the selected mean reversion implies (121.0). A second family, Avellaneda–Lee residual reversion on the same universe rule and costs, agrees: Sharpe −0.28 (−1.47 to +0.93) over 10,916 trades. The sample rules out a Sharpe ratio above 0.92 (one-sided 95% bound) but cannot tell 0 from 0.5. The decisive test, on 2026 data, stays sealed until its pre-registration is filed.

1The pre-registered 2026 test Sealed

Every design choice in this study was made on 2020–2025 data, over 33 versions, so the development period cannot test those choices. The decisive test runs on data none of them has seen: the 200 trading days from 2 January to 19 October 2026. No daily price history of any security for dates after 31 December 2025 has been requested, and the fetch code refuses such a request until the registered commit unseals it. The protocol is filed first; each arm then runs once, and its result is published whatever it is.

The power is stated before any 2026 price is seen. With returns shaped like the development period's, a test at Holm's first step (one-sided 2.5%) over 200 days detects with 80% probability only an annualised Sharpe ratio of about 2.96 (3.15 for normal returns), and its power against a true Sharpe ratio of 1, 2 and 3 is 16%, 48% and 81%. The holdout can falsify a large edge; it cannot confirm a small one, and not rejecting the null will not show that the edge is zero, which is why each arm's one-sided upper bound is reported. Running v32 alone first would spend the holdout, so the three arms are frozen together and each runs once.

2Performance in the development period

The main backtest enters trades from 3 July 2023 to 31 December 2025 on $100M of notional capital. Its pairs are formed on the point-in-time index at the end of 2022 and re-formed every 126 trading days on the trailing three years. The 213 trades returned −0.39%, with a maximum drawdown of −4.05%. The book was flat on 2 of 628 days (v32's book, with its regime gate, was flat on 207) and used 26.9% of capital on average, gross, against a 30% cap.

3Is it significant?

No: every bootstrap interval below contains zero, gross or net, in both strategy families. The more useful question is what the data can rule out. With 628 daily observations the standard error of the annualised Sharpe ratio is 0.59, allowing for the returns' fat tails and autocorrelation, so the one-sided 95% upper bound is 0.92, and a true Sharpe ratio would need to be about 1.47 to be detected with 80% probability. A Sharpe ratio of 1.0 is rejected (p = 0.037); 0.5 is not (p = 0.172). A strategy whose true Sharpe ratio is 0.5 needs about 9.5 years of daily data to show as significant; at 1.0, about 2.4. Hurdles that allow for the 32 recorded versions sit higher still: a Sharpe ratio of 1.75 (Bonferroni, one-sided) or 1.78 (t = 3.00, Harvey, Liu and Zhu).

4Why there is no edge

There is no gross edge for costs to remove. Before costs the 213 trades made $1,520.1k; modelled costs were $1,907.4k, 67.4% of it market impact, so costs would have to fall to 0.80 times their modelled level for net P&L to break even, and the gross return per trade is itself indistinguishable from zero (t = −0.25). Four measurements of the evidence layer point to one mechanism: the cointegration the selection finds does not persist beyond what chance produces (Exhibit 13), the rule's edge on the data that selected a pair disappears afterwards (Exhibit 12), and out of sample the spreads move like random walks between the target and the stop, with half-lives longer than the rule allows (Exhibits 11 and 14).

5Execution and costs

Every fill pays its own costs, from the stock's own liquidity on that day, and fills at the next day's close: a signal seen at the close of day t trades in the closing auction of day t+1. The average trade held 15.1 trading days, and its larger leg was 1.61% of that stock's dollar ADV. Annual turnover was 9.0× capital. The cost model was fixed before the run, and its uncertain parts are stressed in scenarios declared in advance.

6Risk and attribution

The book's factor exposures are the ones a convergence strategy built from hedge ratios below one should have. Against the Fama–French five factors, momentum and short- and long-term reversal, with Newey–West errors, the main backtest loads on short-term reversal (t = 4.01), on the market (t = 2.37) and against momentum (t = −3.96). The eight factors explain R² = 0.158 of its daily variance and leave an alpha of −0.51% a year (t = −0.38). The walk-forward loads the same way (short-term reversal t = 3.80) and leaves an alpha of −2.59% a year (t = −2.25). Against an equal-weight sector-ETF proxy the main backtest's beta is 0.037 (t = 2.40).

7Multiple testing

At the first formation, 5,082 same-industry pairs were tested and 489 were “cointegrated” at p < 0.05, against 254.1 expected by chance alone. None survives a Benjamini–Hochberg correction at q = 0.05 (1 at q = 0.10): the excess is spread thinly over many weak relationships. Selection kept 309 pairs at that formation; across all 7 formations, 130 distinct pairs were traded. Beyond the pairs, every trial whose result has been seen is on record.

8Second strategy family: residual reversion

A null on one rule says little about the idea. Avellaneda and Lee (2010) trade each stock's residual against its sector ETF when that residual, modelled as a mean-reverting process, is stretched; many small concurrent bets give the test far more power than 213 pair trades. The parameters are the paper's: a 60-day regression on the sector ETF, an AR(1) fit of the cumulative residual, trades only when mean reversion is faster than 30 days, entry at |s| > 1.25, exits at −0.50 (long) and 0.75 (short). Fills, costs and the universe rule are the pairs strategy's: next-day closes, the same cost model, and the 500 most liquid point-in-time members, re-ranked every 21 trading days. Over the same period it made 10,916 trades, held 181 positions on average and returned −3.37% (Sharpe −0.28; Newey–West p = 0.598). Before costs it earned +5.35 bp a trade (t = 0.87) against 11.30 bp of cost.

9Design and method

Selection sees formation data only; every trading decision on day t uses data up to day t and fills on day t+1; the evidence is computed after the run. Tests change future prices and check that no past decision moves. The v33 design differs from the published v32 design in seven fields, each fixed for a reason that does not depend on 2020–2025 returns.

10Validation

A backtest on real data says what a strategy did, not whether the code measures what it claims. scripts/synthetic_validation.py runs the real engine, with its full cost model, on synthetic markets whose answer is known, 10 markets per scenario; CI runs a smaller version on every push to main and on pull requests.

The test suite adds causality checks, each confirmed by re-introducing the bug it guards against: every decision dated on or before T is unchanged when all prices, volumes and macro data after T are replaced (with classical ranking and with a trained ranker); the same trades occur at the same z-scores when the vendor back-adjusts history for a later split; the anatomy reconstructs every trade's P&L from prices to the cent. The statistics are tested against reference implementations (statsmodels for Newey–West, arch for the Politis–White block length and the model confidence set, an independent CSCV for PBO, the authors' code for Ledoit–Wolf and for the Sharpe-ratio power functions).

13Reproduce

Every number on this page is either a marked value checked against the run's headline JSON or computed from the committed results by one script, scripts/build_site_data.py. The runbooks clear every PAIRS_* switch, fix the hash seed, refuse an environment that differs from requirements.txt and record the commit in every export. A later price fetch will not match the cached data bit for bit, because vendors revise history; the run manifest pins what was used.

# the environment pinned in requirements.txt (Python 3.12)
python3.12 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt

# the development-period studies behind this note, in order:
# main, anatomy, costs, residual, variants, validation, publish
PYTHON=.venv/bin/python bash scripts/run_dev_studies.sh

# this page's data, and the check of every marked number
.venv/bin/python scripts/build_site_data.py           # results/v33_dev
.venv/bin/python scripts/check_published_numbers.py   # README.md, index.html

# the pre-registered 2026 test: once, from a full clone
# at the registered commit, after the pre-registration
PYTHON=.venv/bin/python bash scripts/run_holdout.sh

# view the page locally
python3 -m http.server 8000
